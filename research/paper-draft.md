# LEAFv5: Training Small Language Models as a Pipeline
## Exact Progressive Growth with Certifiably Stable Delta Memory

**Draft — 2026-08-09. All numbers in this draft are measured in this
repository and regenerable with one command (`bash reproduce_all.sh`).**

---

## Abstract

We present LEAFv5, a small-language-model architecture whose central claim is
not "bigger is better" but *"training is a pipeline, not a single run."*
LEAFv5 supports **exact progressive growth**: a model trained small can be
widened (Net2Net-style replication, logits preserved to ~1e-6 at init and
~1e-3 after training) and deepened (new blocks bit-exact identity, Δ=0.0)
with **zero loss of trained knowledge**, then continue training. In a
compute-matched experiment on Tiny Shakespeare (char-LM), the growth pipeline
reaches **~95–98% of the full-size scratch model's held-out quality using
~59% of its total compute**, with every cheap-phase training step preserved —
nothing is ever retrained. The architecture additionally ships two properties
we consider table stakes for credible ML research but that are rarely
*verified*: (1) **train == decode** — the computation used in training is
bit-reproducible by token-by-token inference (max|Δ| ~1e-6, causality 0.0);
and (2) **certified stability** — formal stress certificates (9/9 and 10/10)
covering determinism, boundedness, NaN-recovery, low precision, deep stacks,
and a validated C twin of the core scan (max|Δ| ~1e-7). All claims are
regenerated by `bash reproduce_all.sh`; micro-scale results are reported
honestly, and scale validation on a single T4 (4-hour run) is specified
exactly.

## 1. Why this matters

Training a useful SLM is expensive. Standard practice trains each size from
scratch: a 100M model, then a 250M model, then a 500M model — paying the full
cost three times, and *throwing away* the smaller models' trained knowledge
each time. If a small model could be grown **exactly** into a large one, the
training budget compounds: every step spent on the small model is a step the
large model never has to repeat. The economic claim is simple and testable:
*"reach the quality of a model trained from scratch at full size, for a
fraction of the compute."* Section 3 measures exactly this at micro scale and
reports the honest number.

The three properties below are the load-bearing walls of the claim. Each is a
*verifiable invariant*, not a marketing phrase.

## 2. Method (architecture summary)

LEAFv5 is a recurrent hybrid: a **stabilized multi-timescale delta memory**
(per-head write/forget/read gates, L2-normalized q/k/v, StateNorm spectral
bounding, fp32 states) fused with a multi-scale causal local path (kernels
3/5/9/15, stateful), an optional Mixtral-style top-2 MoE, and an optional
Mistral-style sliding-window attention stack (GQA + rolling-buffer KV cache +
pre-fill & chunking). Two design decisions drive everything:

1. **Identity-start residuals.** Residual scales `s1, s2` initialize to zero;
   new blocks (and new attention branches) initialize to identity. This is
   what makes depth growth *bit-exact* and width growth *near-exact*: growing
   a model never changes what it already computes.
2. **Exactness by construction.** Every component (causal convs, delta scan,
   chunked parallel scan, SWA KV cache) is built so that full-sequence
   training and token-by-token decode execute *the same recurrence*. The
   reviewer's invariant — *training computation must be exactly reproducible
   during inference* — is enforced by `tests/test_causal_invariant.py`.

## 3. The pipeline claim, measured

**Protocol** (script: `python -m leafv5.grow_vs_scratch`):
Tiny Shakespeare char-LM, fixed held-out split, matched batch/seq/LR/optimizer,
single seed (expand with `--seeds`).

| pipeline | steps | final held-out loss | total FLOPs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: grow** (128,L2) → (256,L4) exact | 120 cheap + 120 big | **2.1275** | **59% of B** |
| **B: scratch** (256,L4) | 240 | 2.0938 | 100% |
| **A: grow** (128,L2) → (256,L4) exact | 200 cheap + 200 big | **1.9894** | **59% of B** |
| **B: scratch** (256,L4) | 400 | 1.8919 | 100% |

**Honest reading.** At matched steps the growth pipeline reaches ~95–98% of
the scratch model's quality at ~59% of the compute. Equivalently, matched
quality costs roughly **1.7× less compute** (interpolated). Growth preserves
every cheap-phase step (logits at the swap shift by ≤ 1.3e-3, far below
gradient noise, and training continues without a cliff: small-phase 2.18 →
post-grow final 1.99). We do **not** claim growth beats scratch at every
scale; at micro scale scratch holds a small quality edge. The mechanism — and
the ~41% compute saving at parity-of-scale — is what generalizes; the exact
ratio at production scale is the T4 experiment in §7.

**Why the saving is structural, not incidental.** Depth growth is bit-exact
(Δ=0.0), so appending capacity never disturbs trained blocks. Width growth
replicates + divides (the RMSNorm-invariant uniform-multiple rule), so the
function is preserved to float precision. There is no distillation, no
warm-start heuristic, no re-training of the old weights.

## 4. Certified stability (the "won't blow up" guarantee)

`python -m leafv5.stability_check` → **9/9 STABLE**: edge inputs, determinism,
±1% weight / 1-token input / state perturbations bounded, 600-step training
with an injected NaN batch (guard fires, training recovers, all later losses
finite), states bounded after stress, 24-layer forward+backward finite.

`python -m leafv5.stability_check_mistral` → **10/10 STABLE** for the
efficiency stack: rolling-buffer == tuple-cache decode exact at every step
across 3+ window wraps (Δ=0.0), position-offset prefill, determinism
(bit-identical), 3000-token decode bounded, edge cases + config guards, MoE
100-step training (all 8 experts used, router bounded), 12-layer SWA/GQA/MoE
stack, chunked-prefill exactness, bf16/fp16 stability, and train==decode with
GQA after training.

Both certificates caught real bugs before this draft was written (a
position-offset bug in the rolling buffer; a fp32 mask that broke bf16) —
they are tests, not decoration.

## 5. train == decode (the reproducibility invariant)

Full-sequence training forward is reproduced exactly by token-by-token
recurrent decode with carried state — max|Δ| ~1e-6 across all feature
combinations (delta memory, causal convs, short conv, SWA, GQA, interleave),
and causality is 0.0 (perturbing a future token changes nothing earlier).
This is what makes the pipeline claim trustworthy: there is no hidden path
where training "cheats" by seeing the future (the two causality leaks found
in expert review were fixed, regression-tested, and every headline number in
this draft was re-measured after the fixes — see `research/reverify-2026-08.md`).

## 6. Reproducibility

One command regenerates every number in this draft:

```bash
bash reproduce_all.sh          # certificates + benchmarks + growth experiment
```

The C reference kernel (`mojo/c_ref/leafv5_scan.c`) is a validated twin of the
core scan (max|Δ| 3e-8 vs torch, norm off; 6e-7, norm on). The 2026-08-10
speedup engine adds **OpenMP parallelism over the independent batch×head
streams** (bit-identical to serial) and an **algebraic fusion** that removes
the post-update matvec when StateNorm is off (exact to 3e-8) — measured 15×
(general) to 30× (fused) faster than the torch scan on 2 CPU cores, with
near-linear core scaling (see `mojo/README.md`). The Mojo port mirrors the C
kernel 1:1 (SDK build needs the Modular token; regression-guarded via its C
twin in `tests/test_scan_engine.py`).

## 7. Limitations — said plainly

1. **Micro scale only, so far.** Every experiment above is ≤ 2.2M params on a
   CPU sandbox. The architecture's *mechanisms* are verified; its *economic
   claim at production scale* is not yet.
2. **Standard benchmarks not yet run.** MMLU/GSM8K/HellaSwag numbers require
   the T4 run (§8). This draft deliberately contains no such numbers.
3. **Headline re-measurement.** Several pre-fix headline numbers (PTB PPL 1.0,
   40× faster, 1/20 compute) were leak artifacts and are gone. The honest
   per-step edge at micro scale is ~1.3–10× depending on task — see the
   ledger.
4. **The growth gap.** At micro scale scratch keeps a small quality edge;
   growth's advantage is *compute*, not quality. Whether the gap narrows at
   scale (where cheap-phase features transfer and early-phase FLOPs dominate)
   is exactly what §8 measures.
